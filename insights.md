Customer Churn Analysis — Business Insights

1. Executive Summary

This project analyzes customer churn to identify customer segments with higher observed churn rates and explore potential factors associated with customer cancellations.
The objective is to turn customer and subscription data into actionable recommendations for improving retention.

2. Key Findings

Finding 1: Churn by Subscription Plan
The preliminary analysis suggests that the Basic subscription plan has a higher observed churn rate than the Standard and Premium plans.
Business implication: Investigate pricing, customer expectations, service quality, and plan benefits before deciding on retention actions.

Finding 2: Churn by Contract Type
The preliminary analysis suggests that customers with monthly contracts have a higher observed churn rate than customers with annual contracts.
Business implication: Examine whether contract flexibility, customer tenure, or differences in customer characteristics help explain this pattern.

Finding 3: Customer Support
Support escalations may be associated with customer churn.
Business implication: Investigate escalated complaints, resolution times, and recurring support issues to identify potential improvements.

3. Business Recommendations

Review the experience of Basic-plan customers.
Monitor early signs of customer dissatisfaction.
Investigate escalated support cases.
Evaluate retention initiatives and measure their outcomes.
Track churn rates across customer segments over time.

4. Limitations

The initial export contains only 21 customer records.
Small sample sizes limit the reliability of comparisons.
Missing support and cancellation information may affect results.
Observed associations do not establish causation.
Findings require validation against the source database.

5. Conclusion

The initial analysis identifies subscription plan, contract type, and customer support as areas worth investigating further.
A larger and validated dataset is needed to establish reliable churn patterns and prioritize retention strategies.
